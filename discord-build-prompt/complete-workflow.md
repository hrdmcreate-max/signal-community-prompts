# Complete workflow: make a community server easier to use

This workflow helps an owner make a Discord community easier for members to navigate. It adapts to the server’s purpose and audience; no server, channel map, schedule, or protected area is assumed.

## Understand the community before proposing a layout

Ask the owner what the community is for, who joins, what members should find or do first, which questions staff answer repeatedly, what content needs a home, and which areas must stay protected or unchanged. Confirm who can approve changes and what access the assistant has. If connected access is unavailable, work from a supplied channel list or screenshots rather than claiming to have audited the server.

Start with a read-only review. Note categories, channels, forums, descriptions, pins, key resources, activity, and visible permission boundaries. Record anything inaccessible or ambiguous. Do not expose restricted content or private conversations in a public summary.

## Show the whole proposal first

Group member needs into a small number of recognizable areas. Give each category and channel a clear purpose, an audience-friendly name, and a concise topic explaining what belongs there and where to begin. Consider welcome, core activity, help, shared work, announcements, events, or archives only where they fit the community.

For every proposed change, show the current location, proposed location or name, reason, and anything that could confuse members or break a link. Identify pinned guides and old discussions that should remain accessible. Name protected areas in the private audit and preserve their content, access, and boundaries unless the owner specifically includes them in the approved scope.

Present a complete before-and-after map before making changes. Separate cosmetic cleanup, moves, new channels, permission edits, and archiving so the owner can approve each area clearly. Ask unresolved questions together. An audit or recommendation alone is not permission to reorganize the server.

## Apply only approved changes

After approval of a concrete map and scope, make small, reversible changes in batches. Preserve history and existing content. Prefer moving a channel or updating a topic over deleting it. Do not remove roles, access boundaries, history, or channels without specific approval. Check the exact destination immediately before each change.

Create a welcome path that lets a new member take one quick first step. Point to a few useful places by their actual channel names. Preserve accessibility options, existing rule history, event details, and any protected spaces. Do not invent links to channels that have not been verified.

## Verify the result

Recheck category order, names, topics, forum tags, pinned guides, permissions, and key links. Confirm protected areas remain untouched. Check that a typical member can find the welcome path and the resources they need without being exposed to private information. Provide a concise before/after record, the approved map, and any change that could not be completed.

Keep recommendations separate from completed actions. An audit does not reorganize a server. Do not claim cleanup, outreach, or permission changes unless they were approved and verified. Use the map as the basis for later improvements.
