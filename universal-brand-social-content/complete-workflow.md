# How the image and posting system is built

This is a brand-neutral explanation of the workflow behind the master prompt. It covers the creative decisions and the practical checks that turn generated images into a reliable first-month schedule.

## 1. Start with a fact sheet and a visual identity

A strong image prompt starts with accurate information. Separate verified website facts, current owner-approved facts, and unresolved details. Check prices, deadlines, scope, discounts, service area, and CTA destination before placing them in an image.

Do not assume that a mailing address is a customer-facing office. Do not turn an unclear discount into a calculated price. When scope is uncertain, an approved invitation to ask about the discount can communicate the opportunity without inventing eligibility or stacking rules.

Record the visual identity in concrete terms:

| Input | What the agent needs |
|---|---|
| Logo | Actual file, approved variants, clear-space rules, acceptable backgrounds |
| Palette | Color values if available, dominant/accent/neutral relationships |
| Typography | Font or character: geometric, editorial, rounded, condensed, etc. |
| Voice | Clear examples of how the brand sounds and words it avoids |
| Audience | Who is buying, their situation, and the action the post should invite |
| Proof | Authorized real screenshots, product images, testimonials, or case studies |
| Restrictions | One-use subjects, unsuitable scenes, prohibited claims, visual boundaries |

Color variety should come from an approved palette and compatible neutrals. A consistent brand does not require every image to be drenched in one color.

## 2. Give every image one message

Choose one approved benefit and one action. A useful layout often has:

- A short, large headline.
- One brief supporting sentence.
- A recognizable subject or product in an authentic scene.
- A clear CTA.
- The real logo and display URL.
- Necessary conditions in a small, readable footer.

Three to eight headline words and one short support line are useful starting constraints, not absolute rules. Important conditions may need multiple centered lines. If the design is crowded, shorten the copy before reducing legibility.

The display URL in the image is a visual reminder. The caption's destination is the actual link route where the platform supports it.

## 3. The prompt structure

The complete blueprint is included in the master prompt. Its sections each solve a different problem.

| Section | Purpose | Example using placeholders |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose/audience | Keeps the creative tied to a business outcome | Encourage [audience] to request [approved action] |
| Reference roles | Prevents conflicting or ambiguous image references | Logo for mark; identity guide for palette; photo for product |
| Scene/backdrop | Replaces generic imagery with a specific believable setting | [Business type] doorway with morning light and real materials |
| Subject/action | Makes each asset distinct and relevant | [Age/appearance/clothing] naturally [specific action] |
| Realism | Addresses common generation artifacts | Natural skin, coherent light, believable grip and tool use |
| Composition | Specifies where the message and subject belong | Subject to right; negative space left; CTA in foreground |
| Exact copy | Reduces spelling and claim drift | Quote headline, support, CTA, URL, and footer verbatim |
| Hierarchy/safe area | Keeps the ad usable in a mobile feed | Dominant headline; readable smaller terms; safe edge margins |
| Finish/constraints | Defines the actual deliverable | One finished ad; no invented words, claims, or contact sheet |

The structure moves from the scene and subject into the specific design and constraints. It replaces vague requests such as “make it look expensive” with controllable details: lighting, camera distance, texture, composition, type scale, spacing, and restrained brand elements.

## 4. Turn a rough idea into a complete image prompt

Use this enhancement instruction when the idea is still vague:

> Turn the following concept into one precise commercial image-generation brief for the supplied brand. Preserve all approved facts and exact copy. Specify the audience, one message, scene, distinct subject or product, natural action, lighting, composition, brand references, typography hierarchy, CTA, URL, necessary footer, safe margins, and realism constraints. Resolve contradictions and remove repetitive adjectives. Do not invent claims, prices, offers, identities, or readable interface details. Return the complete final prompt and flag any essential missing fact before generation.

Then provide the concept, brand identity, exact copy, approved conditions, reference files, and platform format. The agent should show an image sample for approval rather than generate an entire month from an untested direction.

For a product brand, replace the person description with an exact product reference, material, scale, setting, and natural use context. For illustration, describe the approved illustration language and keep copy/identity constraints. Do not mechanically append photorealism to an illustration brief.

## 6. Make the month varied while preserving the brand

Create a subject and style ledger before production. Each row describes the person/product, setting, action, framing, light, palette balance, headline treatment, ratio, and content pillar.

Possible directions include:

- A confident owner entering a business, photographed in a wide environmental frame.
- A focused person working at a desk with convincing materials and hand placement.
- A craftsperson actively making or fitting something, with a closer crop.
- A person speaking with a customer, with a useful conversational moment.
- A product or service detail with generous negative space.
- A bright neutral editorial layout with restrained accent color.
- A darker cinematic layout with the same logo, copy voice, and hierarchy.
- A community scene suitable for a genuine approved community offer.

These are creative variables, not demographic formulas. Include varied people naturally and avoid stereotypes. When a person is permitted only once, track that constraint explicitly. Generated scenes are illustrative; they do not establish that the people are actual clients or staff.

Use portrait, square, and wider artwork only after checking the intended network's current format specifications and previews. The final image's actual dimensions may differ from the requested size, so inspect the file.

## 7. Generate directly and correct precisely

Generate each finished advertisement with an image tool. Typography and design elements belong in the image-generation brief for this workflow. File-management scripts may rename, inventory, validate, and package assets; they should not become an alternate coded ad compositor.

One complete prompt per unique image allows distinct casting and scenes. Maintain the approved art direction as reusable text, then add the specific scene and exact copy for that asset.

A narrow edit is preferable when the whole image already works:

~~~text
Make one precise edit to this finished advertisement.
Replace ONLY [identified line/element] with EXACTLY "[replacement]".
Preserve the people/product, photograph, logo, palette, headline,
CTA, URL, layout, dimensions, and all other text.
The replacement must have [specific punctuation/alignment].
Make no other changes.
~~~

Inspect the edited result. Image tools can retain the original typo or change unrelated details. Keep versioned originals and upload only the final verified revision.

If a required logo cannot be rendered accurately, report it and obtain an approved alternative. Exact-reference instructions reduce drift but do not guarantee a pixel-perfect mark.

## 8. The creative quality check

Check every final image at full size and at phone-feed size:

1. Read every letter of the headline, support, CTA, URL, dates, prices, and conditions.
2. Check punctuation, line breaks, centered footer alignment, contrast, edge margins, and visual hierarchy.
3. Compare the logo and palette with the supplied references.
4. Inspect face, anatomy, hands, objects, shadows, product details, and any visible screens.
5. Verify that all claims and conditions match the approved fact sheet.
6. Confirm that the asset and caption correspond to the same offer and destination.
7. Verify the actual file dimensions, supported format, filename/version, and image uniqueness.

OCR is a useful helper, but a clean OCR result does not prove that a face, logo, hand, or layout is correct. The lead agent must review delegated QA results and actually inspect the deliverables.

If all images pass, create a clean final folder. Keep failed versions elsewhere. A hash identifies the exact file revision and helps prevent uploading the wrong image.

## 9. Captions and platform adaptation

Use an audience-appropriate opening, relevant context, one CTA, applicable conditions, a few useful hashtags, and the approved destination at the bottom when appropriate.

For LinkedIn, tie the message to a professional need or practical business decision. Specify personal profile versus company Page. A company's voice and an individual's voice can differ.

For other platforms, adapt length, crop, tone, link route, and format to the network. Do not call an Instagram caption URL clickable. Use the verified available route and avoid assuming permission to change a bio or account setting.

No invented testimonials, fake website screenshots, fabricated results, or expired promotions. Real screenshots require permission and should show the actual approved product or website rather than a generated imitation.

## 12. Tests and measurement

Choose a few useful hypotheses before generating a large batch. For example:

| Hypothesis | What changes | Keep reasonably comparable | Useful evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| People help a service brand | Person versus service/product detail | Offer, CTA, audience context, approximate timing | Click/engagement rates and inquiry quality |
| A question invites conversation | Question versus benefit headline | Visual direction and destination | Responses, clicks, qualified inquiries |
| A palette treatment attracts attention | Warm neutral versus dark brand treatment | Message, CTA, subject relevance | Reach-normalized engagement and clicks |

Organic comparisons are observational. Changes in distribution, timing, topic, and audience make causal claims unreliable, particularly with small samples.

Paid A/B tests need a separately authorized budget, a defined objective, a controlled variable, suitable delivery conditions, and enough data to support the conclusion. Small budgets may support a useful directional test without proving a winner. Do not split a small budget across a large number of variants or promise a number of leads.

Budget arithmetic is explicit: total authorized campaign budget ÷ run days gives the average daily allowance, before considering platform-specific minimums and campaign settings. Keep production costs, subscriptions, and ad spend separate.

Use verified website analytics and conversion tracking when available. Tracking access and permissions are checked rather than assumed. Platform recommendation scores are inputs; actual business outcomes and budget boundaries remain the decision criteria.

Review at week one, midway, and month end. A review calendar is not a running automation. Set up scheduled monitoring only when requested and supported, and verify that it exists.

Meta describes A/B testing as an experiment process; apply its current guidance when a Meta paid test is within scope. [Meta Blueprint A/B testing guidance](https://www.facebookblueprint.com/student/activity/639591-optimize-campaign-performance-with-ab-testing)

## 13. Native verification and recovery

A reliable posting workflow records every destination separately:

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Content ID | Connects image, caption, calendar, and platform record |
| Account name/URL/ID | Prevents wrong-brand or wrong-profile posting |
| Final media revision/hash | Proves which approved file was selected |
| Full caption and link | Allows exact comparison after saving |
| Datetime and named time zone | Avoids ambiguous local times |
| Scheduler and publication mode | Distinguishes automatic publishing from a reminder |
| Status | Separates drafted, submitted, scheduled, and actually published |
| Native ID/URL | Enables follow-up when the platform exposes it |
| Confirmation and proof | Establishes that the saved result exists |
| Verified time and errors | Makes recovery and handoff practical |

After each submission, read the confirmation. At the end, reload the native queue and check all intended rows, destination networks, media, full captions, dates, and times. Save screenshots with enough context to show the actual account and schedule.

When an action times out, check the ledger and queue before resubmitting. The action may have completed even when the tool did not return. Leave unavailable IDs blank; do not derive post IDs from image filenames.

Future posts are **scheduled**, not published. A saved draft is not scheduled. An upload is not a published post. The completion report should say exactly which state each destination reached.

For a new platform later, reuse approved assets when suitable, adapt captions and formats, audit the new destination and plan, check the existing queue, and create new per-destination records. Do not blindly duplicate the whole previous schedule or inherit its publishing authority.
