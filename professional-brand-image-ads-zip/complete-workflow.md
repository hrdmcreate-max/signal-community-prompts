# First-month production and testing playbook

A first month should test a few useful creative ideas while staying coherent and useful to the audience. This is a planning framework, not a claim about universally optimal posting frequency or guaranteed lead generation.

## Define the actual month

Choose either 30 consecutive dates or a named calendar month. Do not use them interchangeably. Start in the future, after required review and feasible account setup. Specify a named zone and local posting times.

Count:

~~~text
content slots = number of dates × posts per date
destination deliveries = sum of deliveries to each selected account
unique assets = distinct creatives actually produced
quota demand = the service’s actual units, split by its quota periods
~~~

Example: 30 dates × one daily slot × three platforms can mean 30 content slots, 30 reusable images, and 90 destination deliveries. Adapted captions do not automatically save quota.

## Starting content mix

Adjust to the brand; these counts illustrate a 30-asset plan.

| Pillar | Assets | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Core benefit / product or service | 8 | Explain value clearly |
| Useful guidance / objection / FAQ | 6 | Address a real customer decision |
| Process / genuine approved proof | 5 | Show how the brand works |
| Human / community / local context | 4 | Make relevance concrete |
| Active truthful offer | 4 | Invite a specific action |
| Direct invitation / recap | 3 | Encourage the next step |

If no offer or proof is approved, reassign those slots to truthful service explanation. Do not manufacture proof to fill a matrix.

A second daily slot is not mandatory. Capacity and audience relevance should justify it. If a second slot is selected, define its separate purpose rather than posting two price posters every day.

## Six layout families

| Family | Visible decisions | Suitable use |
|---|---|---|
| Human manifesto | Strong person/action, oversized type, limited copy | Service benefit / ownership / confidence |
| Bright editorial | Natural light, neutral background, restrained accents | Approachable service/product context |
| Architectural depth | Doorframe/window/surface creates type planes | Movement, entering a business, next step |
| Close-up detail | Real craft/product detail and clean text zone | Quality/process |
| Genuine proof insert | Actual authorized screenshot/photo, faithful content | Portfolio/process proof |
| Focused offer | Short price/action message with generous terms zone | Active approved promotions |

Keep core identity/CTA/type relationships consistent across families. A product brand can replace human subjects with exact real product use/display; an illustration brand should use its approved visual language.

## Copy discipline

One image has one core message and one CTA. Essential conditions remain accurate and readable. Captions can supply supporting context, but cannot contradict an image’s price or conceal a misleading headline.

A footer may be omitted if no conditions are relevant to the creative. When conditions matter, do not hide them. Never append a dense generic disclaimer merely to imitate another campaign.

Do not invent testimonials, outcomes, scarcity, nonprofit terms, or locations. Generated people are illustrative, not proof.

## Organic style comparison

Define two or three hypotheses before production:

| Hypothesis | Variable | Keep reasonably comparable |
|---|---|---|
| Brighter scenes may invite more clicks | Bright neutral vs dark brand treatment | Message, CTA, approximate slot/audience |
| A visible natural action may increase attention | Environmental action vs seated portrait | Offer, core copy, destination |
| Different headline frames may invite inquiry | Question vs clear benefit | Visual family, CTA, audience context |

Tag each asset with one style-test label. Avoid changing five variables and claiming the result isolates one.

Compare equal elapsed windows after publication where possible. Separate platforms and account types. Note different topics, audience exposure, timing, organic distribution, and small samples.

Track absolute counts alongside rates:
- Reach and impressions are different denominators.
- Engagement rate must name its definition, such as engagements / reach × 100.
- Click rate must name its definition, such as link clicks / impressions × 100.
- Never divide by zero or treat unavailable metrics as zero.
- Website visits, inquiries, qualified leads, and sales require actual attribution/access.
- A click is not automatically a qualified lead.
- A saved schedule has no engagement yet.

At early checkpoints, describe directional patterns. “No reliable winner yet” is a valid outcome. A strong-looking creative with low exposure should not be declared a loser.

## Tracking structure

If authorized, use consistent public UTM values:

~~~text
utm_source=[network]
utm_medium=organic_social
utm_campaign=[brand_run_period]
utm_content=[asset_id_or_layout]
~~~

Use URL-safe values and preserve existing query parameters. Verify the complete link and actual analytics route. Never put personal data, secrets, or private account identifiers in tracking URLs.

A platform connection does not install conversion tracking. Do not claim inquiry or lead attribution works until it is tested and visible in the appropriate authorized reporting tool.

## Paid experiments remain separate

Preparing image advertisements is organic creative production by default. A paid test needs a separately approved objective, audience, destination, variants, budget cap, run period, tracking, and review rule.

For a small budget, focus on a few variants and useful directional evidence. Do not promise a fixed number of leads, statistical significance, or a winning ad. Recommendation scores do not replace business outcomes.

Use current first-party platform guidance when a paid test is actually in scope; verify eligibility and minimums live. Do not launch ads while merely preparing this organic prompt workflow.

## End-of-month decision

Keep the brand anchors. Reuse winning principles rather than blindly repeating one face. Prefer the styles/messages associated with meaningful measured actions, while acknowledging limitations.

Report:
- What ran and on which accounts.
- Actual counts, rates, denominators, and attribution limitations.
- Which creative patterns are promising.
- What should be tested again or dropped.
- The next proposed batch and its capacity/budget.
- What approval is required for any expanded scope.

A review plan is not a live monitoring automation. Install and verify an actual recurring job only when requested.
