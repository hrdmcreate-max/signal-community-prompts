# Complete workflow: cinematic social video production

This package helps you plan, produce, review, and—only when separately authorized—prepare a cinematic social video for your own project. It is a workflow prompt for an AI assistant with access to the tools you choose; a text-only chat can plan and draft but cannot operate your files or accounts.

## Start with your project

Read the current request and any materials the user deliberately provides. Establish what the video should help the audience understand or do, who it is for, the intended destination and format, the target length, the delivery date, the available source media, the selected production tools, and the budget or credit limit. Ask one compact set of questions for the important gaps. Do not assume an earlier project's brand, account, voice, model, path, budget, or approval carries forward.

Clarify whether this is a new video or a revision. For a revision, identify the exact approved master and the requested change. Preserve the original and change only what was requested. An audio-only change should reuse the approved picture wherever feasible; do not regenerate motion or upscale again without a reason.

## Plan before production

Review the supplied brief, approved assets, project notes, and existing jobs. Confirm which source files are authorized for this project, who owns them, and any licensing or privacy limits. Keep credentials out of prompts and logs. Record an output location that will not unexpectedly trigger an automated watcher.

Develop a short concept, audience takeaway, tone, and call to action. Create a scene-by-scene storyboard with shot purpose, action, framing, lighting, transition, spoken words, overlays, and estimated duration. Keep scenes visually distinct while preserving continuity in people, props, wardrobe, setting, and color. Critical titles, logos, prices, and URLs should use verified supplied copy and controlled overlays rather than relying on generated lettering.

Check current capabilities, model names, export formats, licensing, and costs for the chosen tools before work that could consume credits. Prefer tools and assets already available. If a paid generation step is proposed, state its cost or uncertainty and wait for the budget authorization required for that job. Review storyboard frames together before paid motion when the user requests that checkpoint. Preserve shots already approved and revise only the rejected pieces.

## Produce and edit

Use the requested aspect ratio and duration. If the platform requirements are unclear or time-sensitive, verify them with an official source. Choose resolution and frame rate that meet the destination without unnecessary cost. Give each generated scene one concrete action and a specific camera/light/environment direction; avoid changing multiple variables at once when correcting a shot.

Assemble the project in the selected editor. Keep picture, narration, music, effects, captions, and on-screen text in separate editable layers when the tools allow it. Use licensed or user-provided audio. Balance narration so it remains clear; duck music and effects under speech. Replace a superseded narration cleanly instead of stacking voices. Keep captions inside safe areas and synchronized with the spoken words.

Use local editing or deterministic animation where it can achieve the requested result with less cost and better repeatability. Generate or upscale only when the chosen workflow actually needs it. Maintain versioned project files, source references, prompts, export settings, and estimated versus actual costs. Do not subscribe, purchase, boost, or publish because a workflow mentions a service.

## Review the finished export

Check the actual rendered file rather than trusting export settings. Verify duration, dimensions, frame rate, codec, audio presence and sync, caption placement, and the final frame. Review representative early, middle, and late frames, then inspect every scene for continuity, artifacts, unsafe margins, typos, and unintended private information. Confirm every title, logo, link, claim, and offer against supplied approved facts. Check the complete audio mix and voice identity when a voice change was requested; do not claim to have listened if only technical checks were possible.

For an audio-only correction, compare the approved and revised picture streams to confirm the visual portion did not change. Preserve the previous final, give the new export an unambiguous revision name, and record a file hash so the reviewed file is the one delivered.

## Delivery and publication boundary

Deliver the finished file, a concise production record, actual cost information when available, and unresolved issues. Distinguish draft, rendered, reviewed, uploaded, processing, scheduled, and verified-public states.

Uploading or publishing is a separate action. If the user specifically authorizes it, verify the signed-in identity, exact destination, current post history, approved caption, disclosure, link, and time zone before the final action. Never substitute another brand or account. Reconcile ambiguous submissions before retrying, and report a post as live only after checking the destination. Otherwise stop with the verified local deliverable.

## Reusable handoff record

For each run, retain a compact brief, storyboard, approved source-asset list, scene prompts, revision notes, export settings, cost record, quality checklist, file hash, and any authorized destination receipt. Use the included production brief and review checklist to make a new run reproducible without carrying over old permissions.
