# Complete workflow: explain an app or bot

Use this workflow to make a tutorial that helps a specific audience complete one task in an app, bot, or software product. It is tool-neutral: the reader supplies the project, product, audience, and intended outcome. Recording, animation, narration, and editing depend on the tools they select.

## Choose the lesson

Start with the learner’s goal: what should someone be able to do after the video? Pick one task and one audience. Identify the product name and version, starting state, prerequisites, expected result, and any information that must be hidden. If the product changes often, record its version and the date so the lesson can be checked later.

Ask for missing context together. Confirm whether the user wants a screen-recorded tutorial, animated explainer, presenter-led lesson, or a combination. Reuse approved visuals, voice, host, motion rig, or an existing lesson kit only when they belong to this project and are authorized.

## Build a lesson people can follow

Outline the lesson in this order: a short promise, what the learner needs before starting, where to begin, the steps in sequence, what success looks like, and one next action. Remove side paths. Split a task that is too large into independently useful lessons.

Write a spoken script in plain language. Use the same labels the learner sees on screen, explain unfamiliar terms the first time, and keep each instruction close to the action it describes. Leave pauses for someone to catch up. Do not promise an outcome the app cannot demonstrate.

Prepare a cue sheet mapping each spoken line to its screen state, cursor action, highlight, zoom, host expression, gesture, or transition. Keep the interface demonstration and any animated host on separate tracks when the editor supports it, so one can be revised without rebuilding the other. Avoid motion that competes with the instruction.

## Record or animate

Capture the correct product version and a clean starting state. Use a test account or safe sample data where possible. Hide customer details, private messages, authentication codes, keys, and unrelated browser content. Do not include real private records just to make a tutorial look realistic.

Record at a resolution and frame rate supported by the destination and editor. Keep the pointer deliberate and visible; pause after important clicks. Use zooms and callouts only to clarify the current step. If using a host, preserve the approved design and align expressions or gestures to actual speech. A voice-only change should reuse approved picture when feasible.

Set the sequence length to the actual narration duration. Check captions against the script, keep controls visible, and avoid placing content behind crop or subtitle areas. Use music or effects only when they help the lesson and are authorized for that use.

## Review and deliver

Play the complete timeline and seek forward and backward. Confirm that the opening matches the task, instructions follow the visible interface, and the final state proves the learner’s goal. Check caption spelling, speech sync, audio clarity, aspect ratio, safe areas, and any transparency needed for compositing. MP4 does not preserve transparency; choose a format supported by the editor and verify it there.

Review representative frames and the exported file. Save a versioned output, script, cue sheet, asset list, export settings, and a brief quality record. Keep source material and the reusable base kit intact. Deliver the video or draft and state missing access, files, or unresolved version details.

## Publication is optional

A tutorial can be planned, recorded, and delivered locally without publishing. If the user separately authorizes a post, verify the exact account and destination, caption, disclosure, and schedule in the live platform. Confirm the post only after checking the destination. A saved draft or uploaded file is not proof of a public result.
