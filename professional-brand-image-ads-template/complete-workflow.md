# Complete workflow: design one brand ad

This universal workflow turns one approved message or offer into a single image-ad concept, production brief, and review checklist. Replace bracketed fields with your own details. It does not assume a particular brand, location, price, platform, or image tool.

## Start with the project

Before drafting, gather only what matters for this ad:

- Business, project, or cause: `[PROJECT_OR_BUSINESS]`
- Audience and desired next step: `[AUDIENCE]`, `[GOAL_OR_CTA]`
- One approved message or offer: `[MESSAGE_OR_OFFER]`
- Facts and claims that can be supported: `[APPROVED_FACTS]`
- Brand references, logo, colors, and examples: `[AVAILABLE_ASSETS]`
- Placement and size: `[PLATFORM_AND_RATIO]`
- Available tools, accounts, time, and budget: `[TOOLS_AND_LIMITS]`
- Content, people, details, or destinations that must stay protected: `[PROTECTED_ITEMS]`

Ask the user focused questions for missing details that would change the concept or make a claim unsafe. Do not invent audience research, prices, deadlines, customer results, permissions, or product features. If a detail is unknown, omit it or label it for confirmation.

## The image brief

Copy and adapt this brief for one image:

```text
Create one original image advertisement for [PROJECT_OR_BUSINESS].

Audience: [AUDIENCE]
Goal: [GOAL_OR_CTA]
One message: [MESSAGE_OR_OFFER]
Supported facts and exact terms: [APPROVED_FACTS]
Placement and ratio: [PLATFORM_AND_RATIO]
Available brand references and their roles: [AVAILABLE_ASSETS]
Visual direction: [SCENE, SUBJECT OR OBJECT, ACTION, LIGHT, COLOR, AND COMPOSITION]
Exact headline: [HEADLINE]
Supporting line: [SUPPORTING_COPY_OR_NONE]
Call to action: [CTA]
Required terms or disclosure: [REQUIRED_TERMS_OR_NONE]
Protected items and exclusions: [PROTECTED_ITEMS]

Use supplied references only for their stated purpose: identity, style, or factual product evidence. Do not copy another brand's identity or invent a testimonial, interface, result, endorsement, offer, or readable background text. Keep essential wording legible at the intended display size. If the image tool cannot render exact text reliably, say so and offer a practical text-layout alternative before producing a final.

Return one complete concept and note any assumptions that need approval. Do not publish, connect accounts, spend money, or alter files unless that action is separately requested and authorized.
```

## A reliable design sequence

1. **Choose one outcome.** Keep the image focused on one audience, one message, and one next step. Move extra explanation to the caption or landing page.
2. **Check the claim.** Tie every price, feature, result, deadline, comparison, and qualification to current supplied evidence. Mark anything stale or unsupported for confirmation.
3. **Assign reference roles.** Say whether each asset is a logo, style example, product screenshot, or source of exact copy. A style reference is not permission to reuse its text, face, composition, or offer.
4. **Plan the frame.** Pick one useful scene or product view, a clear focal point, deliberate negative space, and a reading path from headline to support to CTA. Match the requested placement and ratio.
5. **Set visual anchors.** Use the provided palette, type character, logo treatment, and tone. Vary scene, framing, lighting, and layout intentionally across a series rather than changing identity at random.
6. **Write exact copy first.** Check spelling, punctuation, line breaks, URL, terms, and CTA. Keep the number of words short enough to read on a phone.
7. **Generate one sample.** Use an available tool only after confirming its access and any cost. Show the actual output for the review agreed with the user; do not describe a draft as approved.
8. **Inspect and repair narrowly.** Check every word, mark, face or product, hand, screen, background sign, edge, contrast, and crop at full size and intended feed size. Repair one defect at a time and recheck the whole image.
9. **Prepare delivery.** Keep approved finals separate from rejected drafts. Record source assets, prompt, dimensions, file name, review status, and any remaining issue. A generated image is not automatically uploaded or scheduled.

## Copy and layout checklist

- The headline communicates the main point without requiring tiny text.
- The support line adds useful context rather than repeating the headline.
- The CTA names a real, available next step.
- Any offer includes its current scope, price, conditions, and end date when needed.
- A footer or disclosure is readable, complete, and not hidden by decoration.
- Links and contact details are supplied and verified by the project owner.
- No extra words appear on signs, screens, clothing, packaging, or paperwork.
- No unapproved customer details, private messages, analytics, or account screens appear.
- The final crop preserves the logo, key message, CTA, and required terms.

## Optional caption outline

Use only if a caption is requested:

1. One sentence that connects the audience to the real need.
2. A concise explanation of the verified offer or message.
3. The chosen next step and a supplied, verified destination.
4. Required disclosures or qualifications.

Do not add hashtags, urgency, discounts, links, or performance claims by default. Match the platform’s current format and the user's preferred voice.

## Review and handoff

Agree at the start whether the user wants to review a concept, every final, or a defined batch. Ask again if the work materially changes, a claim or destination is uncertain, or the next action is outside the agreed scope. Keep generation, account access, paid services, ad spend, file edits, and publication within their actual tool permissions and authorization.

A useful handoff names the file, its dimensions, what was checked, what remains uncertain, and whether it is a draft, approved final, or delivered item. Never claim that a prompt alone creates an ad, verifies a claim, grants account access, or publishes anything.
